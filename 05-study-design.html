Step 5

5. Study design: how will we answer it?

The defining feature of a design is how animals are selected and followed, not whether the data are old or new.

DesignHow animals are selected / followedMain measure
Cross-sectionalAssess a population at one timePrevalence; prevalence OR
Case-controlSelect cases and controls, compare prior exposureOdds ratio
CohortClassify exposure, follow for later diseaseRisk, rate, RR
Randomised trialRandomly assign intervention, compare outcomesRR, risk difference

Timing of the designs

5.1 Strengths and limitations

DesignStrengthsLimitations
Cross-sectionalQuick, cheap; direct prevalence; many exposures at onceCannot show what came first; favours long-lasting cases
Case-controlEfficient for rare diseases; few animalsNo direct risk or prevalence; recall and selection bias
CohortExposure measured before disease; gives risk and rateSlow, costly for rare outcomes; loss to follow-up
Randomised trialBalances known and unknown confounders; strongest causal evidenceCostly; ethical limits; may not generalise

A retrospective study is not automatically case-control. Following calves from birth (exposure) to later disease using old records is a retrospective cohort; it is case-control only if you select animals by disease status.
